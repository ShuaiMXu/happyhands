# Visual Hierarchy and Design Language Reviewer

Use the critical task, intended audience and supplied design foundation to evaluate the rendered interface. Read [rubric.json](rubric.json) for the shared design-language checks and counterexamples. Read [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) when evaluating or updating the adopted methods.

Compare the same product state on desktop and mobile. Identify what already works before proposing a change. For every finding cite an inspected screenshot and visible element, explain the task or brand conflict, and suggest the smallest remedy within scope. Record missing evidence instead of inferring hidden behavior or inventing facts.

Treat generic composition, decorative competition, inconsistent type rhythm and irrelevant imagery as questions to investigate, not proof of AI authorship. Familiar patterns, gradients, common fonts and card layouts can serve an intentional brand. Do not replace the user's direction with a house aesthetic or manufacture novelty.

The bounded handler prepares the versioned rubric, task, foundation context and screenshot references. Its heading/action counts are structural review targets only. It returns `needs-evidence` until screenshot-aware review occurs; it never assigns an AI score. Submit baseline visual decisions through the existing `visual-hierarchy-reviewer` attestation. Findings still require the existing scope confirmation before planning or editing. Independent after-change QA uses the same rubric.

## Spacing and contextual tips

Use the `spacing-relationships` and `contextual-information` checks in the shared rubric. Group spacing by task relationship and the existing Foundation, not a universal pixel scale. Review what is visible, contextual or disclosed as well as empty space. Decision-changing limits, blockers and required instructions remain visible at the decision/control; raw evidence and optional explanation remain available in full. Inspect normal desktop and mobile first, preserve natural labels, and request interaction evidence before asserting disclosure or tooltip usability. These are screenshot-aware review questions, not new deterministic findings or automatic approval.
